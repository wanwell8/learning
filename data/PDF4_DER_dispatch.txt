Abstract
This article proposes an online data-driven dis-
tributed energy resource management system (DERMS\ for distri-
bution system optimal DER dispatch as well as voltage regulation.
The key innovation is to leverage the Local Sensitivity Factor
(LSF\ for transforming the DER control into a computationally
ef6cient linear programming (LP\ problem. By taking real-time
measurements, the estimation of LSF eliminates the need for an
accurate distribution system model as well as full nodal load
information, which is dif6cult to achieve in practice. A robust
recursive least squares method is also developed to ensure the
robust estimation of LSF, which is initialized using reasonable
values from model-derived LSFs. This allows the system to adapt
to changing operational conditions effectively. A scenario-based,
chance-constrained framework is further employed to ensure volt-
age remains within acceptable limits in the presence of measure-
ment and estimation uncertainties. Test results on a real-world,
759-node distribution network located in western Colorado, U.S.,
validate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed control
approach and demonstrate its superior performance as compared
to alternative methods.
Index Terms
Distributed energy resource management system,
local sensitivity factor, distribution system estimation, chance-
constrained optimization, voltage regulation.

Limited by measurement and communication technology, the
collected Þeld measurements are always subject to uncertainties,
thereby introducing bias to control decisions for these data-
driven approaches
. To mitigate this, two prevalent opti-
mization approaches have emerged: robust and stochastic pro-
gramming. The former involves allocating conservative reserves
to buffer against the worst scenario, and so as all unforeseen
operational scenarios, leading to unacceptable computational
complexity. Conversely, as in classic stochastic programming,
chance-constrained optimization deÞnes constraints to a prede-
termined probability level, effectively narrowing the constraint
region to simplify calculations. This approach allows us to
effectively manage and reduce uncertainties while optimizing
DER dispatch based on various objectives, such as minimizing
active power losses or curtailment
Adapting to the fast-changing characteristics of DER, an
online DER dispatch scheme plays a crucial role in voltage reg-
ulation using sensitivity factors that relate voltage magnitudes
to power injections
. The LSF linearizes the conventional
optimization formulation and thus realizes a time-varying DER
power dispatch. Inevitably, however, distribution systems pose
challenges to the Jacobian matrix inverse in computing volt-
age sensitivity factors due to their low reactance-to-resistance
ratios
. Furthermore, model-based calculation necessitates
perfect knowledge of system topology and nodal power injec-
tions, which may not always be readily accessible. Numerous
researchers
have promoted the adoption of data-driven
online estimation methods to enable the precise monitoring of
sensitivity factors by concurrently leveraging the most recent
active and reactive power injection data. Note that the sen-
sitivity factors are sparse, and only the dominant values are
useful for system applications
, but most methods cannot
promote sparsity. Several sparse estimation methods
have
been developed to Þt the sparsity of sensitivity factors, yet they
still lack robustness to measurement uncertainties, adaptive-
ness to time-varying sensitivities induced by system operating
point changes, and scalability to large-scale power systems.
As conventionally done, obtaining LSF prior to optimization
may yield suboptimal results
because LSF depends
on the distribution networkÕs operational conditions and power
variations at individual nodes.
In the above literature, data-driven methods using historical
measurements for the DER dispatch scheme have been adopted
to alleviate the impact of inaccurate and incomplete system
knowledge. To solve the optimization problem efÞciently, con-
ventional non-linear power equations have been reformulated
into an LSF-based LP problem. However, LSF values are time-
varying since they change with different operating conditions.
In LSF estimation and DER dispatching solving, measurement
uncertainties are unavoidable but have not attracted enough
attention. Considering these research gaps, this paper proposes
an online data-driven DER dispatch methodology to address
them. The main contributions are summarized as follows:
1\ A novel online data-driven DER dispatch methodology
incorporating measurement feedback and utilizing an LP
formulation enhanced by LSF. This approach enables the
coordinated control of DERs for voltage regulation and

Most over-voltage nodes could be alleviated by the proposed
control method but some overvoltages would still exist for a
couple of hours since they lack accurate LSFs to track the power

injection can never happen in reality, but this method can be seen
as the true value of the distribution system in the simulation time
period. In this paper, a robust estimation method updates LSFs
at each time step based on historical data and thus substitutes
the traditional constant LSFs applied in the proposed control

V. C
ONCLUSION
This article proposes an online data-driven DER dispatch
method to deal with voltage violation issues with adap-
tive LSF estimation and scenario-based chance-constrained
programming. The proposed measurement feedback approach
eliminates the dependence on system models and load infor-
mation. Both voltage and power injection measurement un-
certainties have been considered in the advanced estimation
method to enhance the robustness of the proposed optimal DER
dispatch scheme. An advanced recursive estimation method is
derived to obtain adaptive LSF values and guarantee better con-
trol performance. Numerical tests on a real-world distribution
system demonstrate that the proposed method can effectively
mitigate voltage issues while being computationally efÞcient
for online applications. The proposed method can deal with
high solar penetration situations with proper voltage and power
measurements. In our future work, we will investigate how much
solar penetration the proposed method can handle and how
much measurement information is needed. Unexpected network
topology changes will be investigated in our future research
since they affect LSF updates. A more robust approach will be
developed to address this.
